A short checklist for your first conversation

You don’t need a complete campaign plan to get started. A few answers can help keep the discussion focused on the people you want to reach and the materials you may need.

  • What do you want customers or event visitors to notice or do?
  • Where will they encounter your business or message?
  • Are you preparing for a storefront, a promotion, or a trade show?
  • Which materials do you already have, such as a logo or existing copy?
  • What details must appear, and when do you need the items?

If you’re unsure about the format, describe the situation first. Someone preparing for an exhibit may need several coordinated pieces, while a business improving its storefront may have a more focused sign project. Mistakes to avoid when choosing materials

Try not to begin with a long list of products before settling on the main message. Too many pieces can make a project harder to organize, and a crowded sign or display may leave viewers unsure what to remember. Keep the core information in view, then consider which supporting items make sense for the audience and setting.

Also, don’t treat every format as interchangeable. A flyer gives someone room to read details, while a sign often needs to communicate quickly. A backdrop can be seen from a distance, and a business card is meant to be kept. Check names, contact information, and event details before approving print files or sign artwork.
