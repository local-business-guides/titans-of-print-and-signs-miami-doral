Commercial printer Doral: get business print jobs ready

Before sending a business print job to production, check the artwork, quantities and intended use. This guide covers what to prepare and what to confirm.

Decide what the printed piece needs to do

A Commercial printer Doral business can support may produce materials for customer handouts, office use or a trade show. Start by naming the purpose of each item. A flyer might explain a service or promotion, while a business card needs to make contact details easy to read. Displays and table covers have to suit the booth where they’ll be used.

Think about who will handle the item and where they’ll see it. If people will read it while walking by, keep the main message easy to spot. If they’ll take it home, make sure the details they’ll need later are included. A clear purpose helps guide choices about format, content and design.

A short preflight checklist

Gather the basics before asking for production help. This can prevent avoidable revisions and make it easier to discuss the job:

  • Final wording, with names, phone numbers and dates checked
  • Your current logo and any supporting artwork
  • The quantity you need for each item
  • The finished dimensions or display space available
  • Your preferred paper or display format, if known
  • The date you need the materials and where they’ll be used

If you’re preparing a trade show, make a list of every piece you need. Titans of Print and Signs Miami can produce displays, table covers, apparel, promotional products, backdrops, step and repeats, business cards and flyers. Confirm how each item will be used and packed so the production choices fit the event.

What to confirm before approving a job

Ask how the artwork should be supplied and whether it needs any changes before printing. Review a proof carefully, including small text, contact details, colour choices and image placement. A design that looks right on a screen may need adjustment for its final size or format, so raise concerns before approving production.

Confirm the quantity and specifications for each piece in writing. For several different items, check that the names and versions are easy to tell apart. Ask what the production schedule looks like for your requested date, especially if the materials are part of an event plan. Don’t assume every item will be ready at the same time unless that has been discussed.

Avoid last-minute surprises

One common mistake is sending files before the content has been approved by everyone who needs to review it. Another is ordering a standard format without checking that it fits the intended display area. Check event dates, spelling and contact information before files go to production, and leave time for design review if the artwork isn’t finished.
